## Factor Results

The U23 programme was reached, not bypassed. Its two contrasts record about
`19.5m` minutes, `55.9k..56.1k` fixtures and more than `216k` appearances,
with zero missing or invalid player-month. Despite that volume:

- paired direction holds in only `1/7` worlds in each lifecycle background;

- gap reductions are mixed and below the frozen `0.5` floor;

## Interpretation

The U23 hypothesis was too local. Giving promoted players regular low-detail
football changes development opportunity, but does not reliably create the
quality or product opportunity required to displace opening seniors. The
lifecycle candidate moves the visible output in the right aggregate direction
without enough paired-world coherence or magnitude. Combining two
non-material factors improves some age summaries but lowers generated leader
share and remains far outside every absolute band; it is not an adoptable fix.

The next question must decompose the **quality-to-first-team transition** on
the current product, season by season: how much potential room exists, how much
growth is realised before 23, whether a qualified successor receives senior
starts, and whether the incumbent's role quality actually declines. It must not
retry generic growth, reserve minutes, direct age/output penalties or another
unattributed combined candidate.
